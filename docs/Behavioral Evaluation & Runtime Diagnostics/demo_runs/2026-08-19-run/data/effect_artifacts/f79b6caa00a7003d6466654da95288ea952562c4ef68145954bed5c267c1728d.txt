Homology (biology) - Wikipedia Jump to content From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia Shared ancestry between a pair of structures or genes in different taxa The principle of homology: The biological relationships (shown by colours) of the bones in the forelimbs of vertebrates were used by Charles Darwin as an argument in favor of evolution . In biology , homology is similarity in anatomical structures or genes between organisms of different taxa due to shared ancestry , regardless of current functional differences. Evolutionary biology explains homologous structures as retained heredity from a common ancestor after having been subjected to adaptive modifications for different purposes as the result of natural selection . The term was first applied to biology in a non-evolutionary context by the anatomist Richard Owen in 1843. Homology was later explained by Charles Darwin 's theory of evolution in 1859, but had been observed before this from Aristotle's biology onwards, and it was explicitly analysed by Pierre Belon in 1555.
