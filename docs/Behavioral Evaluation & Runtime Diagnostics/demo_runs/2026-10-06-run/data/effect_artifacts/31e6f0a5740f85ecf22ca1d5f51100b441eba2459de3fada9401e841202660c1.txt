This can be a real-world problem, such as computing the orbits of the planets in the Solar System , or a problem of a more abstract nature, such as Hilbert's problems . It can also be a problem referring to the nature of mathematics itself, such as Russell's Paradox . Such questions are usually more difficult to solve than regular mathematical exercises like "5 − 3", even if one knows the mathematics required to solve the problem. Known as word problems , they are used in mathematics education to teach students to connect real-world situations to the abstract language of mathematics and to motivate learning mathematics by making links to real life.
