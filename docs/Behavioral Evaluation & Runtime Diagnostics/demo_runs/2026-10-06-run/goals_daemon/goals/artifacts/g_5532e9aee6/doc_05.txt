He is known for his work in number theory and arithmetic statistics . He was a junior fellow in the Harvard Society of Fellows and later joined the artificial-intelligence company Anthropic as a member of the technical staff. [ 1 ] [ 2 ] In 2025, he and collaborators gave a second proof that Hilbert's tenth problem has a negative answer over the ring of integers of every algebraic number field , a result originally established by Koymans and Pagano in 2024. [ 3 ] [ 4 ] On July 19, 2026, Alpöge presented an explicit counterexample to the Jacobian conjecture in three-dimensional space, stating that it was discovered using the Claude Fable 5 AI model. [ 5 ] [ 6 ] He announced a counterexample to the Carathéodory conjecture on August 19, 2026 using the model. Four days later, he announced a positive answer to Hopf's problem concerning the existence of a complex structure on a 6-dimensional sphere using AI tools. [ 7 ] His AI-assisted work on Navier–Stokes existence and smoothness with Tristan Buckmaster [ 8 ] was cited by OpenAI in their solution to the Millennium Prize Problem on September 8, 2026. [ 9 ] [ 10 ] Early life and education [ edit ] Levent Alpöge in 2018 Alpöge was born on April 1, 1992, and grew up on Long Island , New York, where he attended Half Hollow Hills High School West in Dix Hills, New York . [ 1 ] [ 11 ] He holds dual United States and Turkish citizenship. [ 1 ] In 2010, he was a finalist in the Intel Science Talent Search for a computer-science project on the algorithmic analysis of MRI scans of blood vessels. His earliest publication, on a CUDA -optimized vessel-detection method, dates from the same period. [ 11 ] [ 1 ] He earned a Barry Goldwater Scholarship . [ 1 ] [ 12 ] The main-belt asteroid 25898 Alpöge is named after him. [ 13 ] Alpöge studied at Harvard University , where he received a Bachelor of Arts in mathematics and a Master of Arts in physics in 2014, graduating summa cum laude . [ 1 ] His undergraduate thesis, The average elliptic curve has few integral points , was supervised by Jacob Tsimerman . [ 1 ] [ 12 ] [ 14 ] After earning a graduate fellowship from the National Science Foundation , Alpöge studied Part III of the Mathematical Tripos at the University of Cambridge as a Churchill Scholar , [ 15 ] earning a Master of Advanced Studies degree in 2015. [ 12 ] [ 16 ] He received his PhD from Princeton University in 2020 with a dissertation titled Points on Curves , supervised by Manjul Bhargava . [ 1 ] Career [ edit ] After completing his doctorate, Alpöge held an NSF postdoctoral fellowship affiliated with Columbia University and Harvard from 2020, and was a Junior Fellow in the Harvard Society of Fellows from 2021 to 2025. [ 1 ] [ 17 ] He subsequently joined the artificial-intelligence company Anthropic as a member of the technical staff. [ 2 ] Research [ edit ] Alpöge works in number theory and arithmetic statistics, including the study of rational and integral points on elliptic and higher-genus curves, Selmer groups , and the distribution of arithmetic invariants of number fields. [ 1 ] Sums of two rational cubes [ edit ] With Manjul Bhargava and Ari Shnidman, Alpöge established the first proven bounds on the proportion of integers that can be written as a sum of two cubes of rational numbers . Their 2022 work showed that at most about five-sixths of integers are so expressible, combining geometry of numbers techniques with the circle method . [ 17 ] The upper bound for the density of integers that are so expressible has been subsequently improved to 68.05% by Peter Koymans and Alexander Smith. [ 18 ] Hilbert's tenth problem over number fields [ edit ] In a paper published in Inventiones Mathematicae in 2025, Alpöge, Bhargava, Wei Ho , and Ari Shnidman proved that for any quadratic extension of number fields K / F {\displaystyle K/F} there exists an abelian variety over F {\displaystyle F} of positive rank whose rank does not grow after base change to K {\displaystyle K} . This implies that Hilbert's tenth problem has a negative solution over the ring of integers of every algebraic number field—that is, there is no algorithm to decide whether a polynomial equation has solutions in that ring. [ 3 ] The result gave an alternative completion of a program relating Hilbert's tenth problem to the rank stability of elliptic curves and abelian varieties initiated by Bjorn Poonen , Barry Mazur , Karl Rubin , and Alexandra Shlapentokh; the Mazur--Rubin-Poonen-Shlapentokh program had been completed in 2024 by Peter Koymans and Carlo Pagano, using instead elliptic curves. [ 4 ] [ 19 ] AI assisted research [ edit ] Jacobian conjecture counterexample [ edit ] In July 2026, Alpöge announced on the social-media platform X that, using Anthropic's Claude Fable 5 large language model , he had found a counterexample to the Jacobian conjecture , a problem in algebraic geometry begun by Ludwig Kraus in 1884 and formally posed by Ott-Heinrich Keller in 1939. [ 20 ] [ 21 ] The example is a polynomial map from three-dimensional complex space to itself whose Jacobian determinant is the nonzero constant −2 but which is not injective, disproving the conjecture in dimensions three and above. The two-variable case of the conjecture remains open. The announcement was made outside of formal peer review , and the arithmetic of the example was independently checked by other mathematicians in the days that followed. [ 2 ] Hadamard matrices [ edit ] On August 12, 2026, Alpöge announced on X that, together with Philippe Voinov and Saul Reynolds-Haertle and using Anthropic's Claude, he had constructed Hadamard matrices for all twelve orders below 2000 for which no such matrix had previously been known: 668, 716, 892, 1132, 1244, 1388, 1436, 1676, 1772, 1916, 1948 and 1964. [ 22 ] [ 23 ] Order 668 had been the smallest open case of the Hadamard conjecture since 2005. [ 22 ] Carathéodory conjecture counterexample [ edit ] On August 19, 2026, he announced an explicit C ∞ {\displaystyle C^{\infty }} counterexample for the Carathéodory conjecture checked with John-Paul Smith and Anthropic's artificial-intelligence system Claude. [ 24 ] Once again the result was announced in a tweet [ 25 ] , but on this occasion Alpoge offered a more comprehensive backstory, asserting that "i literally wanted to just read about it but i did see the complicated literature situation and so in hopes i could help asked him if it was ok to look at with a model" before confirming that "i did wanna have it recorded somewhere public though" and then giving the example. Hopf's problem [ edit ] Alpöge announced an answer to Hopf's problem on X on August 23, 2026, stating that he constructed a complex manifold structure on a 6-dimensional sphere using one of Anthropic's models. [ 26 ] [ 7 ] The same day Alpöge published a 100-page document containing a proof of the positive solution on his personal website. [ 27 ] . Proposed formalizations by Dean Cureton [ 28 ] and by Boris Alexeev [ 29 ] were subsequently posted on GitHub , and Philip Engel published some notes about the proof. [ 30 ] Manon Bischoff [ de ] reported on the finding in Spektrum der Wissenschaft , the German language edition of Scientific American . [ 31 ] Navier–Stokes existence and smoothness [ edit ] Main article: Navier–Stokes priority controversy Alpöge worked with New York University mathematician Tristan Buckmaster [ de ] on the Navier–Stokes existence and smoothness problem, one of the seven Millennium Prize Problems . On August 15, 2026, the pair proved that the Euler equations , the frictionless counterpart of Navier–Stokes, can "blow up" in finite time, extending a forcing technique developed by Diego Córdoba [ eu ] and Luis Martínez-Zoroa [ eu ] . [ 8 ] After verifying the proof in Lean , the pair published a preprint establishing finite-time blow-up with smooth forcing for the incompressible porous medium equation, the 2D Boussinesq system, and the 3D incompressible Euler equations, using Anthropic's Claude and OpenAI's Codex in the work. [ 8 ] After word of the pair's progress reached OpenAI, a team led by OpenAI scientist Sébastien Bubeck used an internal model to find a solution for Navier–Stokes equations. [ 32 ] On September 8, 2026, OpenAI announced a full solution to the problem that resembled the approach used by the pair. [ 9 ] Buckmaster suggested OpenAI researchers may have accessed private work he had stored in a Codex session before publication.
