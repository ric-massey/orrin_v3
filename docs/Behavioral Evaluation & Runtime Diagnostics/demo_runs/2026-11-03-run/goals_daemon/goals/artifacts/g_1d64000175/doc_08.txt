White Born ( 1953-08-28 ) August 28, 1953 (age 73) Oceanside, New York , U.S. Education Emory University (BS, 1974) Medical College of Georgia (MD, 1978) Occupations Preventive cardiologist, clinical pharmacologist, hypertension specialist Employer University of Connecticut School of Medicine (1978–2019) Known for Ambulatory blood pressure monitoring research and applications; cardiovascular outcomes research, EXAMINE trial; CARES trial; INFINITY trial William B. White (born August 28, 1953) is an American hypertension specialist and clinical pharmacologist . His research established that 24-hour ambulatory blood pressure measurement is the clinically relevant determinant of cardiac end-organ damage in hypertensive patients rather than the doctor's office measurement. [ 1 ] [ 2 ] Early life and education [ edit ] White was born in Oceanside, New York. He completed undergraduate studies at Emory University in Atlanta, graduating magna cum laude with a Bachelor of Science in biology and chemistry in 1974; he was elected to Phi Beta Kappa. His MD came from the Medical College of Georgia in Augusta in 1978. [ 3 ] He completed residency training in internal medicine at UConn-affiliated hospitals in Farmington and Hartford, serving as chief medical resident from 1980 to 1981. A fellowship in clinical pharmacology at UConn SOM followed. In 1988–1989 he undertook research training in cardiovascular medicine at the University of Bergen School of Medicine in Norway, focused on invasive hemodynamics and long-term drug effects on hypertension. [ 3 ] Academic career [ edit ] White joined UConn SOM as assistant professor in 1981, was promoted to associate professor in 1986, and received a tenured full professorship in 1991. He served as chief of the Division of Internal Medicine from 1991 to 1997, then led the Division of Hypertension and Clinical Pharmacology in the Calhoun Cardiology Center from 1997 to 2019. [ 4 ] In 2000 he founded the Clinical Trials Unit at the University of Connecticut Health Center, directing it until 2009. He has held the title of professor emeritus in the Cardiology Center since 2019. [ 5 ] Within the American Society of Hypertension (ASH), White served on the Board of Directors from 2006 to 2016, chaired the Public Policy and Advocacy Committee from 2008 to 2012, and served as president from 2012 to 2014. [ 6 ] Research [ edit ] Ambulatory blood pressure monitoring [ edit ] White's early research addressed whether 24-hour out-of-office blood pressure provided clinical information beyond standard office blood pressure readings. A 1989 paper in JAMA demonstrated that average daily ambulatory blood pressure—not clinic pressure—was the primary determinant of cardiac structure and function in patients with untreated hypertension, a finding that contributed to subsequent validation of ambulatory monitoring as a diagnostic tool. [ 1 ] A companion study in the same year found that average daily blood pressure load was a determinant of cardiac function in patients with stage 1–2 hypertension. [ 7 ] In addition, White was among the first investigators to promote ambulatory blood pressure monitoring in the development of antihypertensive therapies, [ 4 ] now a standard in the FDA guidance for evaluation of antihypertensive drugs and the pressor effects of non-cardiac drugs. [ 8 ] Cerebrovascular disease and ambulatory blood pressure [ edit ] A 2011 study published in Circulation established that average daily ambulatory blood pressure was associated with progression of white matter hyperintensity lesions on MRI and with cognitive decline in adults aged 75–89 years with hypertension independent of clinic readings. [ 9 ] INFINITY trial [ edit ] The INFINITY (Intensive versus Standard Ambulatory Blood Pressure Lowering to Lessen Functional Decline in the Elderly) trial was a single-center randomized 3-year trial at the University of Connecticut Health Center, funded by the National Institute on Aging (NIH grant R01 AG022092). It enrolled 199 adults aged 75 and older who had white matter hyperintensity lesions on MRI. Participants were assigned to intensive or standard ambulatory systolic blood pressure targets guided by 24-hour monitoring rather than clinic values. [ 10 ] Results published in Circulation in November 2019 found that the intensive treatment arm had significantly less accrual of white matter lesions over three years compared with standard treatment. [ 11 ] [ 12 ] EXAMINE trial [ edit ] White chaired the steering committee of the EXAMINE trial, a multicenter randomized trial enrolling 5,380 patients with type 2 diabetes who were 15 to 90 days post-acute coronary syndrome. The trial evaluated whether the dipeptidyl peptidase-4 inhibitor alogliptin was noninferior to placebo on major adverse cardiovascular events. Results published in the New England Journal of Medicine in 2013 confirmed noninferiority. [ 13 ] A prespecified secondary analysis published in The Lancet in 2015 found that alogliptin was not associated with an increased risk of hospitalization for heart failure compared with placebo. [ 14 ] CARES trial [ edit ] White was principal investigator of the CARES trial, a multicenter randomized trial enrolling 6,190 patients with gout and high cardiovascular risk who required urate-lowering therapy. The trial evaluated whether the xanthine oxidase inhibitor febuxostat was noninferior to allopurinol on major adverse cardiovascular events. Results published in the New England Journal of Medicine in March 2018 confirmed noninferiority for the primary composite endpoint, but deaths from cardiovascular causes and all-cause mortality were higher in the febuxostat group than the allopurinol group. [ 15 ] Novel antihypertensive mechanisms [ edit ] In a 2026 phase 2 randomized trial published in the Journal of the American College of Cardiology , White and colleagues evaluated XXB750, a novel monoclonal antibody agonist of the natriuretic peptide receptor-1, in patients with resistant hypertension. The trial unexpectedly determined that the drug did not produce a reduction in 24-hour ambulatory blood pressure compared with placebo despite increasing the physiologic target, cyclic GMP. [ 16 ] Cardiovascular safety of noncardiac drugs [ edit ] From the early 2000s, White's work included assessing the cardiovascular effects of drugs developed for noncardiac conditions. He served as a core presenter at several U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) advisory committee meetings, including a 2005 joint advisory committee on COX-2 inhibitor safety and a 2008 meeting on febuxostat for gout. His research in this area has covered cardiovascular safety evaluations of COX-2 inhibitors (celecoxib, rofecoxib), NSAIDs (naproxcinod, naproxen), drugs for neurogenic orthostatic hypotension (droxidopa), COPD therapies (roflumilast), testosterone replacement therapies, and gout therapies (febuxostat). [ 17 ] He was chair of the cardiovascular endpoints committee for the CONDOR trial (celecoxib versus diclofenac) and for the CARES trial evaluating febuxostat. He has been an active member of the Cardiac Safety Research Consortium, which develops methodological frameworks for cardiovascular safety assessment in collaboration with the FDA. Editorial and professional roles [ edit ] White founded Blood Pressure Monitoring (Wolters Kluwer/Lippincott Williams Wilkins) in 1995 and served as its editor-in-chief until 2022. He also founded the book series Clinical Hypertension and Vascular Diseases (Springer-Verlag, New York), which has published eleven volumes. He has held editorial board positions at the American Journal of Cardiology , American Journal of Hypertension , American Journal of Medicine , Hypertension , Journal of Clinical Hypertension , and other journals. Since 2017 he has been an editor and author for UpToDate (Wolters Kluwer) in hypertension and nephrology. [ 19 ] Honors and visiting lectureships [ edit ] White has delivered named lectureships at the Barry R. Materson Memorial Lecture at the University of Miami School of Medicine (2026), the Marvin Moser Visiting Professorship at Yale School of Medicine (2016), and a visiting professorship at Vanderbilt University School of Medicine (2015). He received accelerated faculty promotion to both associate professor and full professor at UConn SOM (1986 and 1991) and was elected a Fellow of the American College of Physicians in 1984 and a Fellow of the American Society of Hypertension in 2010. [ 20 ] Media coverage [ edit ] White's research has been covered in The New York Times , which reported on his work on medical mysteries and hidden emotions in 1999 [ 21 ] and on blood pressure guidelines in 2013. [ 22 ] An interview published in Pharmacy Therapeutics in 2016 described his career and contributions to hypertension research.
