In biology , homology is similarity in anatomical structures or genes between organisms of different taxa due to shared ancestry , regardless of current functional differences. Evolutionary biology explains homologous structures as retained heredity from a common ancestor after having been subjected to adaptive modifications for different purposes as the result of natural selection . The term was first applied to biology in a non-evolutionary context by the anatomist Richard Owen in 1843. Homology was later explained by Charles Darwin 's theory of evolution in 1859, but had been observed before this from Aristotle's biology onwards, and it was explicitly analysed by Pierre Belon in 1555. Sociobiology - Wikipedia Jump to content From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia Subdiscipline of biology regarding social behavior For the book by E. O. Wilson, see Sociobiology: The New Synthesis . Sociobiology is a field of biology that aims to explain social behavior in terms of evolution . It draws from disciplines including ethology and evolutionary biology. Within the study of human societies , sociobiology is closely related to evolutionary anthropology and human behavioral ecology . Sociobiology investigates social behaviors such as the hive society of social insects . It argues that just as selection pressure led to animals evolving useful ways of interacting with the natural environment , so it led to the genetic evolution of advantageous social behavior.
