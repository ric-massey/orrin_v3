It is produced by Stepmates Studios, a Melbourne -based production company, for One Nation , a far-right populist political party in Australia founded and led by Pauline Hanson . Overview [ edit ] Seasons 1 and 2 are set at Parliament House in Canberra , following Hanson (voiced by herself) teaching a class of Australian politicians (voiced by Mark Nicholson ), [ 2 ] [ better source needed ] and parodies current political events while promoting the One Nation party and its policies. The setting in season 3 varies depending on the episode. Episodes are released on One Nation's YouTube channel every Friday and re-broadcast on News24 . The series has been subject to numerous controversies due to its content. The series has also been accused of promoting disinformation. [ 10 ] A report from the University of New South Wales (UNSW) labelled the series " far-right ", which Hanson has denied. [ 11 ] Kurt Sengul and Jordan McSwiney of The Conversation assert that the series is used by Hanson and One Nation to normalise far-right ideas in mainstream culture under the guise of satire, "giv[ing] One Nation greater freedom to express views it would be reluctant to articulate in other settings." [ 5 ] Depictions of political violence [ edit ] The series has come under scrutiny for its portrayals of violence against Australian political figures, particularly in the aftermath of the October 2026 car attack against Independent MP Helen Haines . [ 12 ] Australian Federal Police commissioner Krissy Barrett has linked the content and rhetoric of Pauline Hanson's Please Explain to a rise in threats of violence against elected officials. [ 13 ] [ 14 ] [ 15 ] "Political Self-Destruction" [ edit ] The episode "Political Self-Destruction" was condemned by the Australian Labor Party (ALP) for portraying Albanese murdering Foreign Minister Penny Wong with a knife. [ 16 ] "Under The Bus" [ edit ] The episode "Under The Bus", published in 2023, shows Independent Senator Lidia Thorpe being run over by a bus driven by Pauline Hanson. Thorpe accused One Nation of platforming death threats against her, and said Hanson should be "thinking carefully about the example she is setting". Hanson refused to apologise to Thorpe and responded on Twitter that the cartoon will not be taken down. [ 15 ] [ 17 ] "The State of Melbourne" [ edit ] An episode titled "The State of Melbourne" was originally published in October 2024, depicting Albanese and former Victorian Premier Daniel Andrews being run over by a vehicle . The episode was subject to renewed scrutiny in October 2026, following an incident where Haines was deliberately run over by a car in Wangaratta . Users on social media called for Hanson and One Nation to take the episode down in light of the attack. Ayesha Jehangir, an academic at the University of New South Wales , asserted that the episode is "deeply disturbing" and "risks trivialising aggression" towards elected officials. [ 18 ] Barrett considered "The State of Melbourne" as an example of content that was coarsening rhetoric and increasing violent threats against politicians. Hanson also refused to remove this episode. [ 15 ] The bus depicted in the cartoon is driven by the character Bluey from the popular children's TV show Bluey . [ 19 ] The intellectual property rights to Bluey (owned by creators Ludo Studio [ 20 ] ) are being pursued. Hugh Marks, ABC managing director, appearing at Senate estimates on 25 October 2026, said that he had sought legal advice and that it was a clear breach of intellectual property , deserving of a cease and desist letter. He added that the use of Bluey in the cartoon was "both shocking and disrespectful to the children of Australia who love that character so much". [ 21 ] The ABC has announced it will take legal action against One Nation in order to have the video taken down. [ 22 ] Other controversial episodes [ edit ] "Voter Fraud" [ edit ] In April 2022, the party was warned by the Australian Electoral Commission (AEC) over the episode "Voter Fraud", where Labor is shown rigging postal votes for the 2022 federal election to get elected (therefore falsely implying electoral fraud ). In the episode, then- Opposition Leader (and later Prime Minister ) Anthony Albanese is seen laying in bed sick with COVID-19 and is offered a soup that contains a live bat by Penny Wong . Wong hands Albanese electoral ballots (including one of his dog Toto), with Wong telling him to fill in the ballots to rig the election in favour of the Labor Party. The episode has been geoblocked in Australia on all of One Nation's official social media accounts upon request from the AEC. The AEC also warned the party for its lack of official authorisation at the end of the cartoons, which has since been added to every episode. [ 23 ] "The NDIS" [ edit ] In April 2023, One Nation was also criticised for the episode "The NDIS", which mocks the National Disability Insurance Scheme (NDIS). In the episode, Opposition Leader Peter Dutton and Deputy Opposition Leader Sussan Ley visit a doctor who tells them that after the Liberal Party was "destroyed in Aston " (in reference to the party's historic loss at the 2023 Aston by-election ), the party has been severely damaged but is now eligible for the NDIS. The doctor presents to them a video featuring Albanese and NDIS Minister Bill Shorten who mention perceivably bad things about the NDIS and repeatedly erroneously call the scheme the "National Disability Insurance Scam" before correcting themselves (implying that the NDIS is a scam). [ 24 ] "Gender Confusion" [ edit ] In September 2024, the episode "Gender Confusion" was re-released, which removed Life Education Australia branding that was present in the original version of the episode. The revised episode's description claimed that "originally, this episode didn't get the attention it deserved due to some behind-the-scenes hiccups", [ 25 ] [ non-primary source needed ] likely as a result of a copyright claim by Life Education Australia. The episode still features the iconic giraffe puppet Healthy Harold , the mascot of Life Education Australia. [ citation needed ] "The State of Queensland" [ edit ] In June 2024, Australian conservationist Robert Irwin 's lawyers threatened to sue Hanson over the episode "The State of Queensland", which criticises the state Labor government's policies on issues such as crime, native title , health and the economy. It features Irwin and cartoon character Bluey , a dog who is the titular character of a popular Australian children's animated television series set in Brisbane , who the Queensland government are using to promote tourism in the state. Hanson refused to take the video down and told Irwin to "lighten up". [ 26 ] "Don't Be Hastie" [ edit ] In September 2026, Liberal MP Andrew Hastie labelled Pauline Hanson and Barnaby Joyce as "lying cowards" over the episode "Don't be Hastie", which labelled him as a "traitor" for giving evidence against military veteran Ben Roberts-Smith in a defamation trial against him . [ 27 ] [ 28 ] Hastie told them to either "own up" to the accusations of being a traitor or delete the video. [ 27 ] Hastie stated that he would not take legal action against the statement, adding that he would not sue someone who was backed financially by Australia's richest person, Gina Rinehart . [ 29 ] Pauline Hanson responded, stating she was "not calling him a traitor to this country" and it was simply a "satirical cartoon". Barnaby Joyce responded by saying that he was just being "emotional" because of the electoral risk One Nation posed on Hastie's electorate, Canning , referencing the Secret Harbour state by-election , which had One Nation win the seat of Secret Harbour , which overlaps Hastie's electorate. [ 28 ] Multiple politicians from different parties have also criticised Hanson's claims against Hastie. Tasmanian Senator Jacqui Lambie claimed that Hanson was an "angry little woman" and an attack on Hastie would mean an attack on all that served. [ 30 ] Opposition Leader Angus Taylor said it was "wrong for the cartoon to stay on the internet" [ 31 ] and Energy Minister Chris Bowen said that Hastie was a "patriot". [ 32 ] The Returned and Services League of Australia also criticised Hanson over the episode. [ 33 ] "The Albo-rithm" [ edit ] An episode released on 17 October 2026 titled "The Albo-rithm" was widely denounced for mocking Jacqui Lambie's physical health. Lambie had experienced vaginal prolapse in August caused by injuries she experienced while serving in armed forces. [ 34 ] The cartoon in question depicts an animated Lambie coughing, implying a prolapse is occurring, [ 35 ] along with "a crude sound effect that ridiculed her condition." [ 36 ] The cartoon was condemned by various politicians across different parties for mocking women's health, [ 37 ] including Independent MP Monique Ryan , Liberal MP Garth Hamilton , Shadow Treasuer Tim Wilson , Science Minister Tim Ayres , [ 35 ] [ 38 ] and Prime Minister Albanese. Lambie condemned the cartoon, stating that it made light of reproductive issues experienced by women, and accused Hanson of adding "hatred" and "spitefulness" to the public sphere. [ 43 ] Colouring book [ edit ] A colouring book based on the series has been created. On 28 October 2026 it was reported that ON staff had handed out copies of the book to children at the annual agricultural show at Mount Gambier , South Australia , [ 44 ] on 23–24 October. [ 45 ] As well as unflattering drawings of politicians and other living people, the book included a picture of Bluey and Robert Irwin being held up by a crocodile wielding a knife. A local parent complained and the matter has been escalated. [ 44 ] Legal action [ edit ] As mentioned above, the animated children's TV character of Bluey has been used in two episodes ("The State of Melbourne" and "The State of Queensland") and a colouring book. On 28 October 2026 the ABC again called on ON to remove the videos, but Hanson denied that they were a breach of intellectual property. The ABC is the licensed holder of copyright in Australia , but as Ludo Studio holds the intellectual property rights, the two organisations would need to work together to take legal action.
