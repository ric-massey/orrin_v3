Awareness is a state of knowing, understanding, or being conscious of something. Its precise definition is disputed and varies across disciplines. Some accounts treat it as a synonym for consciousness , while others characterize it as cognitive access to information that can occur without subjective experience . Awareness plays a central role in everyday life by helping individuals perceive their surroundings, monitor and regulate their behavior, and make information available to other cognitive processes. Awareness can take different objects or forms. External awareness concerns objects in the environment, primarily perceived through the five senses . It contrasts with inner awareness, which encompasses bodily sensations, such as pain , and mental states , such as thoughts . Self-awareness involves recognizing oneself as the object of awareness, whereas meta-awareness is awareness of awareness. Focal awareness encompasses contents at the center of attention , in contrast to peripheral awareness. Theorists also examine awareness in nonhuman animals and the possibility of artificial awareness . To measure awareness, researchers typically use indirect evidence because awareness cannot be directly observed from a third-person perspective. Subjective measures analyze reports from individuals describing their awareness of a stimulus . Objective measures infer awareness from observable behavior, such as task performance, or physiological signals, such as brain activity. Awareness is relevant to many disciplines. Philosophy seeks to determine its essential features, how it arises, what functions it plays, and what it subjectively feels like. It considers theories about the relation between awareness and matter , how conscious mental states differ from unconscious ones, and how awareness acts as a source of knowledge . Psychology examines the conditions under which people become aware of information, how this information influences their behavior, and how mental disorders impair or distort awareness. Clinical medicine is interested in how anesthetics reduce or prevent awareness during painful medical treatments . Neuroscience studies the relation between the brain and awareness, for example, by searching for brain regions and events that accompany awareness . Several religious traditions regard specific forms of awareness as spiritually significant, such as the Buddhist focus on mindfulness as nonjudgmental awareness associated with equanimity. Other relevant fields include biology , computer science , linguistics , sociology , and marketing research . Definition Awareness is a broad term for subjective experience , cognitive access to information , or the ability to notice and understand internal and external events. Its precise definition is disputed and varies by context and discipline. [ 3 ] For example, when a person consciously perceives a tree or hears a voice then the tree or voice becomes a content of their awareness. Awareness is not limited to perceptual objects in the environment but also includes internal states, such as bodily sensations of warmth, emotions , desires , intentions , judgments , and thoughts about abstract ideas. [ 4 ] The expression aware that is often used in a factive meaning, according to which one can be aware only of things that are real. In a broader sense, awareness also encompasses contents that do not accurately represent reality or are not believed to be true, such as imagined scenarios, dreams , and hallucinations . [ 5 ] Which contents enter awareness is influenced by various factors, including the external situation; the strength and novelty of stimuli ; and the individual's attention , goals, expectations, and memories . [ 6 ] The different contents are typically not presented as isolated phenomena but are embedded in a broader context of meaningful patterns, including information about their emotional significance to the individual and practical implications for behavior and decision-making . In this regard, awareness often involves an active interpretation that organizes and evaluates information rather than an impartial, passive representation. [ 7 ] Some characterizations understand awareness as an all-or-nothing phenomenon: it is either fully present or fully absent. Another outlook regards awareness as a gradual state that can arise in different degrees. This view reflects the idea that one can be aware of more or less contents at the same time and that those contents can have varying degrees of clarity, vagueness, and uncertainty. [ 8 ] Awareness contrasts with unawareness, which is a lack of subjective experience or knowledge. Unawareness can apply to specific contents of experience, like when a person is unaware of a background noise, or to a complete absence of awareness, which may be the case during dreamless sleep or coma . [ 9 ] Certain forms of cognition occur without awareness, such as low-level neural processes underlying face recognition and language processing . [ 10 ] Awareness plays a central role in everyday life by helping individuals perceive and respond to their surroundings, making information available to cognitive processes such as reasoning and reflection, and allowing individuals to monitor and regulate their behavior by modifying automatic responses or engaging in deliberate actions . [ 11 ] It is studied in several fields, including philosophy , psychology , and neuroscience . Research is not limited to human awareness but also addresses the awareness of nonhuman animals . [ 12 ] The term awareness originated from the Old English word gewær , which has Germanic roots. The Old English term entered Middle English as the word iwar , which developed into the modern English adjective aware . [ 13 ] The noun awareness was formed by adding the suffix -ness , with the earliest known use in the 1830s in the work of the physician and writer David Macbeth Moir . [ 14 ] Relation to consciousness Awareness is closely related to phenomenal consciousness and the terms are often used as near synonyms. Understood in this sense, awareness is characterized as subjective experience that involves a first-person perspective on oneself and the environment. It is not merely the availability of information but also includes a phenomenal dimension or experiential character of what it feels like to encounter the contents of awareness. [ 15 ] However, the terms awareness and consciousness are not always treated as synonyms, and some theorists explicitly distinguish between them. According to one view, awareness is access to mental contents that makes information available {{harvnb|Gennaro|loc=§ 1. These capacities can include the abilities to respond to environmental stimuli; to discriminate between objects; to store, retrieve, and interpret information; to formulate plans of action; and to report one's mental states. [ 17 ] According to one interpretation, this function-based characterization implies that any biological or artificial system with the relevant capacities can be described as aware, regardless of whether it has phenomenal consciousness. [ 18 ] In this sense, it is possible to distinguish between conscious awareness, which is accompanied by subjective experience, and unconscious awareness, which involves cognitive processes without phenomenal consciousness. For example, the medical condition of blindsight , in which people with brain damage lose visual consciousness but can still respond to certain forms of visual information, is sometimes suggested as an example of unconscious awareness. Attention prioritizes certain objects or features while filtering out irrelevant contents, thereby focusing cognitive resources on aspects considered relevant. Awareness is not limited to the focus of attention and includes peripheral contents that are noticed but remain in the background. For example, a person may focus attention on a laptop while remaining peripherally aware of an ashtray in the corner of their desk. [ 23 ] Wakefulness , another connected concept, is a condition of being alert and responsive to one's environment rather than being asleep , anaesthetized , or in a coma. Awareness is typically associated with wakefulness [ 24 ] but may also occur without it, as when a person is dreaming and aware of the dream scenario. It may also be possible to be awake without being aware; for example, a person in a vegetative state with severe brain damage may still undergo sleep–wake cycles while lacking signs of awareness. [ 25 ] The concept of awareness further overlaps with those of perception, knowledge , and sentience . Perception organizes and interprets sensory information about the world, including visual and auditory impressions. It is a central form of awareness but not the only one, since people can also be aware of internal states, such as emotions and thoughts. [ 26 ] Knowledge is often characterized as a cognitive achievement, such as a true belief based on good reasons. Awareness overlaps with knowledge in this sense and can be a source of knowledge. However, awareness can also present inaccurate contents, such as hallucinations. [ 27 ] Sentience is usually defined as the capacity to experience positive or negative feelings, such as pleasure and pain . {{multiref | {{harvnb|Van Gulick|2014|loc=§ 2.1 Creature Consciousness}} | {{harvnb|Birch|2024|loc=§ 2.1 Sentience and Consciousness}} }} /ref>"}},"i":0}}]}'> [ d ] Awareness can involve sentience in this sense but may also include contents other than feelings. [ 29 ] Forms External and internal Many forms of awareness are discussed in the academic literature, often distinguished by the types of content they present. External awareness, or exteroception, is awareness of one's environment. It is primarily tied to the five senses , which are different modes of perceiving things outside oneself: seeing , hearing , touching, tasting , and smelling . Impressions from these sensory modes are typically integrated into a unified awareness of the objects and events around oneself and their relative spatial locations. External awareness helps individuals gather information about the world, respond to changes in their surroundings, and navigate their environment. [ 30 ] Direct and indirect realists debate whether the contents of external awareness are material objects themselves or representations mediating between perceiver and objects, such as sense data . [ 31 ] Inner awareness encompasses one's own body and mental states . {{harvnb|Farrell|McClelland|2017|p=1}} /ref>"}},"i":0}}]}'> [ e ] Like external awareness, bodily awareness is multimodal, involving diverse senses and elements. They include proprioception associated with the position and movement of one's limbs and other body parts; the vestibular sense covering balance, orientation, and self-location; interoception concerning internal physiological states such as hunger , thirst , breathing , heartbeat , and temperature; pain ; tactile sensations; and a sense of bodily ownership and ability to act. Bodily awareness allows individuals to monitor their internal states, regulate physiological needs, and coordinate their movements. [ 33 ] An influential classification {{harvnb|de Vignemont|2026|loc=§ 1.1 Varieties of Bodily Experiences}} /ref>"}},"i":0}}]}'> [ f ] divides these elements into two categories: bodily sensations, which affect specific regions of the body, such as pain in a foot and an itch behind an ear; and bodily feelings, which are experienced as diffuse states not confined to particular body parts, such as thirst and fatigue. [ 34 ] Another taxonomy contrasts high-level body awareness, which involves conceptual or emotional representations of the body, with low-level body awareness, which involves more immediate sensorimotor information often directly relevant to action. [ 35 ] Müller-Lyer illusion is an example of misleading information in external awareness: the upper horizontal line appears longer than the lower horizontal line because of the attached arrowheads, but both lines have objectively the same length. [ 36 ] Another type of inner awareness concerns knowledge of one's own mental states, including thoughts , memories , beliefs , emotions , desires , intentions , and mental images . Introspection is the process of examining one's mental states, as when a person recognizes that they are imagining a specific scenario, entertaining a particular thought, or feeling anxious about the outcome of an event. Understood in a broad sense, introspection covers all forms of inner awareness of the mind . Understood more narrowly, it is an explicit or reflective form of inner awareness in which a person deliberately attends to psychological events, in contrast to pre-reflective forms of inner awareness, which occur without introspection. [ 37 ] Both external and inner awareness are sources of knowledge . Academic debates assess and compare their reliability. [ 38 ] External awareness can present false information in the form of illusions , as in the Müller-Lyer illusion , in which one line appears longer than another although both have the same length. [ 39 ] A traditionally influential view holds that many forms of inner awareness are immune to error , for instance, that it is impossible to be aware of pain without being in pain. This view has been challenged in the contemporary discourse. One suggested counterexample is that a person may mistake a sensation of coldness for pain, leading them to believe that they are in pain when they are not. A more limited proposal restricts the immunity to a specific type of error: error through misidentification. This view holds that a person cannot be wrong about whether they, rather than someone else, are undergoing the experience. Such errors are possible in external awareness. For example, when a person in an accident sees a broken arm, they may falsely assume that it is their own arm. [ 40 ] Self Understood in one sense, self-awareness is the same as inner awareness, covering all awareness of one's bodily and mental states. In another sense, it involves recognizing oneself as the object of awareness. This implies that one is aware of oneself as oneself, meaning that the content is not only something that belongs to the self but is at the same time recognized as such. [ 41 ] For example, looking at an old photograph of oneself is self-awareness if one recognizes oneself but not if one mistakes the depicted person for a stranger. In this sense, self-awareness is not limited to private aspects, such as personal feelings, but also encompasses awareness of public features, such as one's physical appearance and mannerisms . [ 42 ] Self-awareness can include a temporal dimension, in which a person sees themselves as an entity that persists through time; a social dimension, involving awareness of how one is viewed by others; a narrative dimension, concerning awareness of oneself as an enduring character in stories constructed by oneself and others; and an evaluative dimension, in which a person assesses themselves and their behavior against standards and ideals. [ 43 ] Some philosophers hold that all conscious awareness involves pre-reflective self-awareness: an implicit consciousness of oneself as the subject of experience without the self becoming an explicit content of that experience. Pre-reflective self-awareness in this sense contrasts with reflective introspection in which the self becomes an object of attention. It is controversial whether some form of self-awareness is present in every experience. [ 44 ] Suggested counterexamples of selfless experiences include states of drug-induced ego dissolution through psychoactive substances , flow experiences involving undivided attention to a task, and being fully absorbed in a captivating movie. [ 45 ] Another debate concerns the definition and nature of the self, for instance, whether it is an object distinct from the environment, a specific mental faculty, a conscious observer of changing experiences, or a shifting collection of mental processes without a permanent essence. [ 46 ] Views about the self can influence how self-awareness is understood. For example, David Hume challenged the existence of certain forms of self-awareness, arguing that there is no distinctive experience of a self. He held that instead of a persistent self, there is only a successive bundle of experiences forming a stream of consciousness . [ 47 ] Meta Meta-awareness is awareness of awareness. [ 48 ] Meta-awareness is awareness of awareness. It is a form of metacognition that involves explicit knowledge of what one is thinking, feeling, or perceiving. During episodes of meta-awareness, experience turns back on itself and takes note of its contents. For example, a student may recognize that instead of reviewing their lecture notes as intended, they have spent the past few minutes imagining weekend plans. The episode of mind-wandering was a form of awareness without explicit self-monitoring since the student had no meta-awareness that their attention had drifted. Some forms of mind-wandering may include meta-awareness, which is reflected in the contrast between tuning out and zoning out: a person may intentionally decide to tune out from an ongoing task while remaining meta-aware, whereas unintentional episodes of zoning out are forms of mind-wandering without meta-awareness. [ 48 ] A key function of meta-awareness is to monitor and regulate ongoing mental processes. It can help individuals detect and address problems in other cognitive activities. For instance, meta-awareness can help the student whose mind has wandered to refocus attention on reviewing lecture notes. [ 49 ] Meta-awareness is also relevant to certain meditative practices that cultivate the ability to engage in nonjudgmental observation of mental events, such as mindfulness . [ 50 ] Nonhuman The study of awareness is not limited to humans and also addresses animal awareness . Animals typically detect and respond to features of their environment, suggesting a basic form of awareness of their surroundings. A key question is whether these forms of animal intelligence involve or indicate conscious awareness. Common sense often assumes that at least some species, such as dogs and cats, are conscious. However, scientifically determining whether a particular species is conscious is difficult because consciousness cannot be directly observed.
