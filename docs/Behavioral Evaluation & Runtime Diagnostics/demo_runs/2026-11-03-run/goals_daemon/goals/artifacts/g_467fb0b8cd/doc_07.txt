The precise causes of ADHD are unknown in most individual cases. [ 37 ] [ 38 ] Meta-analyses have shown that the disorder is primarily genetic with a heritability rate of 70–80%, [ 39 ] [ 40 ] [ 41 ] where risk factors are highly accumulative. [ 42 ] The environmental risks are not related to social or familial factors; [ 43 ] [ 44 ] [ 45 ] they exert their effects very early in life, in the prenatal or early postnatal period. [ 7 ] However, in rare cases, ADHD can be caused by a single event including traumatic brain injury , [ 39 ] exposure to biohazards during pregnancy, [ 7 ] or a major genetic mutation. [ 46 ] As it is a neurodevelopmental disorder, there is no biologically distinct adult-onset ADHD except for when ADHD occurs after traumatic brain injury. [ 7 ] [ 47 ] Signs and symptoms Inattention, hyperactivity (restlessness in adults), disruptive behaviour, and impulsivity are common in ADHD. [ 48 ] [ 49 ] [ 50 ] Academic difficulties are frequent, as are problems with relationships.
