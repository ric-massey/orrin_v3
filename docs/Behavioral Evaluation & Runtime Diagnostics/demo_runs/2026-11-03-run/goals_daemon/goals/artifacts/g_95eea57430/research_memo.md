Waste burners were originally cylindrical, and designs varied over time, but most surviving examples are of the conical type, and thus named for their resemblance to beehives , teepees , or wigwams . Burners have an opening at the top that is covered with a steel grill or mesh to keep sparks and glowing embers from escaping. Sawdust and wood scraps are delivered to an opening near the top of the cone by a conveyor belt or Archimedes' screw , where they fall onto the fire near the center of the structure. Wood burners vent smoke and ash into the atmosphere without filtering, causing air pollution .
