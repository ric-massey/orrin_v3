List of mathematical discoveries made with artificial intelligence - Wikipedia Jump to content From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia Beginning in the mid-2020s, large language models , including reasoning models , have made increasing progress in generating mathematical proofs at research level. [ 1 ] [ 2 ] [ 3 ] Most such proofs have been constructed using models from OpenAI and Anthropic . 2021 [ edit ] In February 2021, researchers at the Technion – Israel Institute of Technology published results from the Ramanujan machine , an algorithmic system designed to discover mathematical conjectures. The system generated previously unknown continued fraction representations involving mathematical constants such as π , e , Catalan's constant , and values of the Riemann zeta function . Some of the conjectured identities were proved, while others remained unproven. Unlike an automated theorem prover, the system generated conjectures without independently establishing their validity. [ 4 ] [ 5 ] In December 2021, researchers at Google DeepMind collaborated with mathematicians to apply machine learning to problems in knot theory and representation theory . The research led to the discovery and proof of a new relationship between the algebraic and geometric properties of mathematical knots. It also produced a new formula for computing Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomials , suggesting a possible approach to resolving the combinatorial invariance conjecture for symmetric groups. [ 6 ] 2022 [ edit ] In October 2022, researchers at DeepMind introduced AlphaTensor , a reinforcement learning system that discovered more efficient matrix multiplication algorithms. For 4 × 4 matrices over the finite field with two elements, AlphaTensor found an algorithm requiring 47 scalar multiplications, compared with 49 using two applications of Strassen's algorithm . The system also discovered improved algorithms for other matrix sizes, including algorithms using ordinary arithmetic. [ 7 ] [ 8 ] 2023 [ edit ] In December 2023, researchers at Google DeepMind used a pre-trained LLM to discover a new lower bound for the cap set problem and new algorithms for the bin packing problem . [ 9 ] The results for the cap set problem were verified by mathematician Jordan Ellenberg . [ 10 ] 2024 [ edit ] In October 2024, researchers at Meta and the Ecole des Ponts et Chaussees found pre-trained Seq2seq transformers could discover new global Lyapunov functions at a higher rate than non-AI systems. [ 11 ] 2025 [ edit ] In May 2025, Google DeepMind's AlphaEvolve agent discovered an optimization for the Strassen algorithm , reducing the scalar multiplications needed for 4x4 matrices from 49 to 48. It also improved the lower bounds for 11 kissing numbers . [ 12 ] In September 2025, Math Inc.'s Gauss agent formalized the strong prime number theorem in Lean , producing approximately 25,000 lines of code and formally proving more than 1,000 theorems. [ 13 ] In November 2025, researchers at Chinese universities constructed a reinforcement learning algorithm which improved the lower and upper bounds for 15 kissing numbers . [ 14 ] 2026 [ edit ] Q1 [ edit ] In February 2026, Google DeepMind used its Gemini model to survey the 700 open Erdős problems , which proved four of them and found nine published solutions that had not previously been widely recognized. [ 15 ] In March 2026, Math Inc.'s Gauss agent formalized Maryna Viazovska 's proofs of sphere packing in 8 and 24 dimensions, in approximately 200 thousand lines of Lean code. [ 16 ] [ 17 ] In March 2026, Google DeepMind's AlphaEvolve agent improved lower bounds for nine classical Ramsey numbers . [ 18 ] Q2 [ edit ] In May 2026, Google DeepMind used an AlphaEvolve-like model, adapted for mathematical proofs, to prove nine Erdős problems and 44 out of 492 formalized OEIS conjectures. [ 19 ] Epoch AI then used various LLMs to prove or disprove 147 of these 492 OEIS conjectures. [ 20 ] In May 2026, OpenAI announced that an internal model had produced a disproof of the unit distance problem , [ 21 ] and several mathematicians collaborated to produce a verification and exposition of the result. [ 22 ] In May 2026, Max Grinzstajn prompted GPT-5.5 Pro to discover a counterexample for Borsuk's conjecture in dimension n = 63 , the smallest dimension for which a counterexample is known. [ 75 ] Proofs across 722 papers over a wide range of 377 mathematical topics were self-published in the OpenAI bulk release of mathematical papers . The proofs were generated using an unreleased model. [ 76 ] [ 77 ] Approximately 42% of the papers were accompanied by a Lean formalization. [ 78 ] Three papers were retracted by OpenAI after peer review within three days of publication, and other papers have been criticized for making stronger claims about the problems than were formalized in Lean, or being too mathematically vague or poorly written to prove a statement. [ 78 ] In October 2026, American artificial intelligence companies OpenAI and Anthropic announced disproofs of the Exponential time hypothesis ETH found by internal LLMs, exhibiting explicit algorithms claimed to run in subexponential time. A team of experts, including those who formulated ETH, announced a collaboration to examine this work.
