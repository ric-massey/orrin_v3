[ 3 ] Early developments [ edit ] History of rationalistic theologies [ edit ] Main article: History of philosophy Further information: Metaphysics , Natural theology , and Rationalism Deistic thinking has existed since ancient times ; the roots of Deism can be traced back to the philosophical tradition of Ancient Greece . [ 23 ] The ancient Greek philosopher Xenophanes (6th century BC ) has been described by scholars as having a one-God theology that projects pandeistic ideals. [ 24 ] The ancient Greek philosopher Chrysippus (3rd century BC) affirmed that "the universe itself is God and the universal outpouring of its soul". [ 25 ] The Hellenistic philosophical school of Stoicism , which advocated in favor of epistemology and logic , was founded by Zeno of Citium in the 4th century BC. [ 26 ] [ 27 ] In the 3rd century BC, Chrysippus [ 26 ] shaped much of Stoic theology and cosmology . [ 28 ] For the Stoics, logic was the part of philosophy [ 29 ] to achieve a happy life aligned with nature and the universe. [ 30 ] The Stoics held that an understanding of ethics was impossible without the use of logic. [ 30 ] In the 3rd century AD, the schematic Christian theologian and philosopher Clement of Alexandria explicitly mentioned persons who believed that God was not involved in human affairs, and therefore led what he considered a licentious life. [ 31 ] In the early 8th century AD, the Indian scholar, mystic, and theologian Ādi Śaṅkara promoted the Advaita Vedānta school of Hindu philosophy , founded by Śaṅkara himself, which incorporated the metaphysical speculation of the Upaniṣads . [ 32 ] He stressed that the individual soul or ultimate self ( ātman ) is identical to the eternal, unchanging, and formless Brahman , the Vedic conception of the creator God , who is understood as the real observer and cognizer of the universe. [ 33 ] [ 32 ] [ 34 ] Advaita Vedānta also espouses monism , and holds the impersonal Brahman to be unchanging and undifferentiated from the individual self, ātman .
